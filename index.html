    How scores work

    Transparent math. Honest gaps. Call before you book a monthly.

    Quality (0–10)

    • Review rating 25%
    • Amenities / site quality 20%
    • Noise / setting 15%
    • Security 15%
    • Year-round climate / livability 15%
    • Wifi / livability 10%

    Price + value

    Lower cost scores higher, calibrated to a national monthly (Rio Grande Valley $400 is a 9; SoCal $1,500 is mid-pack). Prefers a published monthly. If monthly is unknown, effective monthly = nightlyFrom × 22 and the row is flagged estimated.

    value index = quality / (effectiveMonthly / 1000) — that is bang for the buck. A 7.0 park at $400/mo outruns an 8.0 park at $2,000.

    Season + rank

    Composite rank is 0.38×quality + 0.32×price + 0.15×fit + 0.15×season. Season score is ~9.5 in a park’s best window, ~2.4 in its avoid window. Year-round SoCal still gets a climate bonus when season is “any.”

    Class C is easy almost everywhere; 14–21 night caps still hurt. Trailer + Tesla wants extra vehicle, unhitch room, and a trailer that can sit. Florida in summer is not “interesting weather” — it is heat, humidity, and hurricane season. Be there Nov–Apr.

      How to actually run this

      This is the country-wide Frontier market scan: live-in parks and storage lots near Frontier airports, scored on a forward schedule (default spring 2027). Nationwide weather ranking — every state, Frontier or not — stays at the site root.

      The 45-minute rule

      Default filter is ≤ 45 minutes to the Frontier airport. Stretch parks (Apache Junction, Colorado Springs, Ocala) stay in the file; clear the drive cap to see them. Last-minute Go Wild is a midnight seat drop plus an Uber, not an hour-twenty drive in the Tesla.

      Sit the rig, or store it

      If a monthly park will let an empty trailer sit, a 2–4 day LA dash costs the Uber round-trip — the month is already sunk. If they will not, or you are not paying monthly, use a storage lot ($80–$360) and Uber. Airport parking is the expensive wrong answer for a 30-foot trailer.

      Frontier rank

      Composite is 0.18×quality + 0.16×price + 0.10×fit + 0.12×season + 0.16×airport drive + 0.12×LAX + 0.10×sit + 0.06×hub. Dash/week trips shift a little more weight onto sit. Uber is an estimate from miles × city multiplier, not a live quote.

        How the rank is built

        Quality, 0–10

          Price

          Price uses the monthly the rank is allowed to use, then multiplies by the stay cap. A real monthly needs 28 nights or month-to-month. A range uses its midpoint. Winter and summer use a 50/50 blend of the two season midpoints, not the cheapest floor. The Rates control switches that to the winter midpoint. No monthly, or a cap under 28 nights, becomes nightly × 30 and is marked ESTIMATE. Caps of 14 nights or less multiply price by 0.25, 15–21 by 0.45, 22–27 by 0.70. Seasons that disagree use the stricter cap. No monthly is invented.

          Fit, distance, and rank

          Class C fit cares about hookups, length, and a stay of at least 28 nights. Trailer + Tesla is a separate score: extra vehicle, easy unhitch or pull-through, staffed or gated, and a real monthly help. No extra vehicle, a tight back-in, weak security, a stay under 28 nights, and a $200-plus nightly resort used as trailer storage hurt it.

          Proximity is road miles to the Pier: full score through 20 miles, then small, medium, and heavy, and 140 miles or more scores 0 and sorts to the bottom with a FAR badge.

          rank = 0.40×quality + 0.30×price + 0.15×fit + 0.15×proximity
